1. What Sentinel Circle is — and isn't​

Sentinel Circle is an assistive monitoring tool. It watches conditions you configure (a Wi-Fi network, a Bluetooth beacon, an email, a notification, or an unrecognized nearby device) and alerts the caregivers you set up when something changes.

Sentinel Circle is not a medical device, a security system, or a substitute for in-person care, supervision or professional medical advice. It is one layer among several you should have in place for someone with declining cognitive function — not the only one. In particular:

  • A sensor can only report what it observed from the phone it runs on. A dead battery, a phone left behind, a disabled permission, a network outage, or Bluetooth being switched off can all mean no alert is sent even though something you'd want to know about has happened.
  • Delivery of an alert (SMS, phone call, email, WhatsApp, or an on-device alarm) depends on services outside our control — your phone carrier, your email provider, WhatsApp, and Android itself — and can fail, be delayed, or be silently blocked by any of them.
  • We strongly recommend using Sentinel Circle alongside a dedicated real-time location-tracking app, not instead of one, and alongside — not instead of — whatever in-person care and supervision arrangements are appropriate for the person's needs.

You are solely responsible for deciding whether Sentinel Circle is an appropriate tool for your situation, and for having other safety measures in place. Read the in-app disclaimer shown at first launch (and again after major updates) before relying on the app for anything.

2. Consent and legal authority to monitor​

Before configuring Sentinel Circle to watch a person's location, devices, or communications, you must have the legal right to do so. What that means depends on the person:

  • If the person is able to understand and decide for themselves, you need their informed consent.
  • If the person is not able to give informed consent — for example because of dementia or another condition affecting their capacity — you must hold legal authority to make this kind of decision on their behalf: a power of attorney that covers their personal care, a court-appointed guardianship, status as their substitute decision-maker, or whatever equivalent your jurisdiction recognizes. Being a relative or a caregiver does not, on its own, give you that authority.

Laws about monitoring another adult, and about who may decide for someone who cannot decide for themselves, vary by jurisdiction. Using the app in a way that isn't legally permitted where you are is your responsibility, not ours. Don't use Sentinel Circle to monitor someone covertly, or in a way that violates their legal rights.
